Privacy

Atmos Gallery runs in your browser. Your images are read on your device, your palettes are never held on a server, and a shared link tells us nothing about who opened it.

Last updated: 12 September 2026 · Data controller: KauStudio ApS

Your images are read on your device

The palette is extracted on your own machine: the image is read into a canvas, clustered in OKLCH, and discarded. The full-size image is never uploaded.

One thing does leave, and it leaves automatically: choosing an image starts the naming, so you are not asked first. Open whatever file you like, at any resolution. What gets sent is a downscaled copy of it, around 960 px on its longest edge, together with the hex values and their shares. Nothing goes with it: no filename, no account, no identifier. We don’t keep it, and Anthropic doesn’t train on it. If that call fails the palette is named locally instead, but the thumbnail has still been sent.

Your palettes stay in your browser

Your library lives in this browser’s local storage, under first-party keys. There is no server holding it and no account attached to it. Clearing your browser data deletes it, so if a palette matters to you, use Back up to keep a copy on your own disk.

Share links carry the palette itself

A shared link encodes the palette in the part of the address after the #, which browsers never send to the server. Until 31 August 2026 our analytics reported the whole address, fragment included, so opening a shared link recorded the palette it carried. It recorded the palette, never who opened it. We now cut the fragment before anything is sent.

What we can see

No accounts, no ads, no cookies at all. The site runs on Vercel, which keeps standard access logs: your IP address and user-agent, as any web server must. Vercel Web Analytics and Speed Insights measure visit counts and loading speed. Both are cookieless, neither identifies you or follows you to other sites, and a tracker blocker stops both without changing how the tool works. We rely on legitimate interests for that. Interpretation is different: it happens because you asked for a palette to be read.

Where it goes, and for how long

Two processors, both in the United States and both under a written agreement: Vercel Inc. hosts the site, Anthropic PBC performs the reading. Those transfers rely on the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and the Standard Contractual Clauses. There is no one else. We keep nothing ourselves: Vercel discards its logs within a day, and Anthropic publishes its retention at privacy.claude.com. Your palettes last as long as you keep them.

Your rights

We hold no file on you. Nothing we process carries a name or an account, and what exists is short lived: our host’s access logs, for about a day, and the thumbnail on its way to be read.

The rights the GDPR gives you still apply: a copy, a correction, deletion, and an objection to anything we base on legitimate interests. Write to hello@kau.studio. In most cases the answer will be that there is nothing to find, because we cannot tie a request to a log entry without more about you than you should have to hand over. You can also complain to Datatilsynet, the Danish Data Protection Agency.
