Privacy

Atmos Gallery runs in your browser. Your images are read on your device, your palettes are never held on a server, and a shared link tells us nothing about who opened it.

Last updated: 1 September 2026 · Data controller: KauStudio ApS

Your images are read on your device

The palette is extracted on your own machine: the image is read into a canvas, clustered in OKLCH, and discarded. The full-size image is never uploaded.

One thing does leave, and it leaves automatically: choosing an image starts the naming, so you are not asked first. A thumbnail of the image, up to about 960 px on its longest edge, together with the hex values and each colour’s share, goes to Anthropic’s API for the reading. Nothing goes with it: no filename, no account, no identifier. We don’t keep it, and Anthropic doesn’t train on it. If that call fails, the tool names the palette locally instead. It does not mean nothing was sent: the failure is usually a reply that did not come back, and by then the thumbnail has already reached us and Anthropic. Only a build with no interpreter configured sends nothing.

Your palettes stay in your browser

Your library lives in this browser’s local storage, under a handful of first-party keys beginning palette-generator. One more sits outside that prefix, animatedGridState, which remembers whether the design grid overlay is switched on and holds nothing about you or your palettes. There is no server holding it and no account attached to it. Clearing your browser data deletes it, so if a palette matters to you, use Back up to keep a copy on your own disk.

Share links carry the palette itself

A shared link encodes the palette in the part of the URL after the #. Browsers never put that part in the request itself. That alone is not enough, because a script can still read it and send it separately, and ours did. Our analytics reported the whole address, fragment included, so opening a shared link recorded the palette in it. That was possible from 25 July 2026, when the analytics went in alongside links that already carried a palette, until 31 August 2026, when we changed it to cut the fragment before anything is sent. Only visits to shared links were affected, and only the palette in the link, never who opened it.

What we can see

No accounts, no ads, no cookies at all. The site runs on Vercel, which keeps standard access logs: your IP address and user-agent, as any web server must. Two cookieless tools measure the site itself: Vercel Web Analytics for visit counts and Speed Insights for loading speed. Neither identifies you, follows you to other sites, or sees anything you do inside the tool. A tracker blocker stops both, and the tool works exactly as before.

We rely on legitimate interests for that, to serve the site and to know roughly how many people use it. Interpretation is different: it happens because you asked for a palette to be read.

Where it goes, and for how long

Two processors, both under a written agreement and both in the United States: Vercel Inc. hosts the site, Anthropic PBC performs the reading. Those transfers rely on the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and the Standard Contractual Clauses. Vercel lists its sub-processors at security.vercel.com. There is no one else: no ad network, no third-party analytics, no CRM, no hosted fonts.

We keep nothing ourselves. Vercel discards the hash that tells visits apart after 24 hours, and its runtime logs after a day; Anthropic publishes its retention at privacy.claude.com. Your palettes last as long as you keep them.
