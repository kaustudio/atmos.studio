What Atmos Gallery is

Atmos Gallery reads a colour palette from an image and describes the mood it finds there. It is made by KauStudio ApS, a company registered in Denmark. It runs in your browser as a desktop tool — you drop in an image, it returns a palette you can name, keep, export and share.

What a palette reading is — and isn't

It is an interpretation, not a measurement

The palette, its name and its description are produced by a reading of the image — clustering in OKLCH plus a descriptive vocabulary. Two people can reasonably disagree with any of it. Treat the words as a starting point for your own judgement, not as a fact about the image.

It is not colour-managed

Colours are read from the image data as your browser decodes it, on a display we know nothing about. Do not rely on Atmos Gallery for colour-critical work — print production, brand matching, manufacturing tolerance. Verify against a managed workflow and a physical proof before anything is committed.

Keeping your own copies

Your archive lives in this browser's local storage. That has a consequence worth stating plainly: clearing your browser data deletes it, and we cannot get it back — there is no server-side copy, by design. If a palette matters to you, save a project file and keep it on your own disk. Losing an archive to cleared browser data is not something we can remedy.

Shared links

A shared link carries the palette inside the link itself, after the #. Anyone with the link can open it. Treat a shared link as public: once you have sent it, you cannot recall it, and we have no record of it to delete.
